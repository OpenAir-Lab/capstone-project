Lab 1 Video Narration Script  
Title: ESP32 Boot, Flashing, and Strapping Pins

---

[Opening]

In this lab, we’re going to walk through the very first thing every student needs to understand before working with the rest of the OpenAir Lab 
radio system: how the ESP32 boots, how to flash firmware onto it, and why the strapping pins matter.

By the end of this video, you’ll know exactly how the ESP32 decides what mode to start in, how to force it into bootloader mode, and how to 
successfully upload your first program.

---

[Section: What Happens When the ESP32 Boots]

When the ESP32 powers on or resets, it performs a very specific sequence.

First, it reads a small group of pins called strapping pins. In our design, these include GPIO0, GPIO2, GPIO5, GPIO12, and GPIO15. 
Their voltages at the moment of reset tell the ESP32 what to do next: whether to load code from flash, enter UART programming mode, 
or configure flash voltage.

Once those pins are sampled, the values are locked in, and the chip proceeds to run the bootloader or the application.

Understanding these pins is important, because if any of them are pulled the wrong way, the ESP32 simply won’t boot or accept new firmware.

---

[Section: EN and BOOT Buttons]

On the MCU modulino you’ll see two buttons: EN and BOOT.

EN is the reset button. Pressing it restarts the ESP32.  
BOOT is connected to GPIO0. Holding this button forces the ESP32 into UART bootloader mode, which is required for flashing code over USB or the
ESP-PROG.

To enter bootloader mode manually, hold BOOT, tap EN, and release BOOT once the flashing process begins. 
You will get used to this sequence quickly.

---

[Section: Connecting the ESP-PROG]

To flash firmware, connect the ESP-PROG to the modulino.

Connect TX from the ESP-PROG to RX on the ESP32.  
Connect RX from the ESP-PROG to TX on the ESP32.  
And don’t forget ground. Without a shared ground, the UART interface will not work.

Once connected, open your serial monitor at 115200 baud and press EN. You should see the ESP32 print its boot message.
This confirms the board is powered, the strapping pins are correct, and the UART interface is functioning.

---

[Section: Uploading Code]

Now that the board is wired and we’ve confirmed the serial output, let’s upload our first test program.

In PlatformIO or the Arduino IDE, open the example that prints “ESP32 Boot Successful” and blinks a GPIO.

Start the upload.  
If the ESP32 does not automatically enter bootloader mode, hold BOOT, tap EN, and release BOOT once the flashing begins.

When the upload finishes, open your serial monitor again.

You should see the printed message.  
You should also see the LED blinking on the assigned GPIO pin.

At this point, your board is fully operational.

---

[Section: Troubleshooting]

If you encounter errors, this is normal. Here are the most common issues:

If you see “Failed to connect: Invalid head of packet,” the ESP32 is not in bootloader mode. Try the BOOT plus EN sequence again and check your
wiring.

If you see random characters in the serial monitor, the baud rate may be wrong, or the flash voltage selected by GPIO12 may be incorrect.

If you see no output at all, verify that the board is actually powered and that the EN pin is pulled high.

Troubleshooting is an essential part of embedded work, so treat these messages as valuable signals rather than failures.
